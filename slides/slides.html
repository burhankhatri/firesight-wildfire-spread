Problem definition

Satellites show where fire is today.
Responders need tomorrow.

  • Task: given today's fire and 11 environmental layers for a 64 × 64 km area (1 km pixels), predict which pixels burn the next day.
  • Why it matters: evacuation, crews, fire breaks. Fires are getting worse as the climate warms.
  • Objectives: clean data → physics-informed features → 4+ tuned models → explain drivers → what-if digital twin.
Dataset · Google Earth Engine

18,545 real US fires, 2012–2020

Next Day Wildfire Spread (Huot et al., IEEE TGRS 2022). Built in GEE from MODIS fire, VIIRS NDVI, SRTM elevation, GRIDMET weather & drought, GPWv4 population. Train / eval / test: 14,979 / 1,877 / 1,689 tiles.

Preprocessing

Imbalanced, cloudy, and noisy

  • Missing values: 2–3% of labels are cloud (−1), so those pixels are removed. Any NaN inputs are median-imputed.
  • Outliers: every layer is clipped to a physical range (e.g. negative rain removed).
  • Normalization: StandardScaler for LR and MLP.
  • Imbalance: only ~1.2% of pixels burn. Train on all fire pixels plus 3× non-fire (half near the fire). Evaluate at the real rate.

Exploratory data analysis

What drives next-day spread

Risk falls fast with distance, rises 1.3–1.9× downwind of a fire, and roughly triples from moist to very dry fuel.

Models & tuning

5 models + a baseline, tuned fairly

  • Logistic Regression · Random Forest · XGBoost · LightGBM · MLP neural net
  • Baseline: persistence (tomorrow = today)
  • RandomizedSearchCV, 3-fold GroupKFold by fire (no leakage between folds), scored by average precision
  • Threshold picked on a separate eval split (7.5 M pixels)
  • Tested on 6.7 million unseen pixels

Original study (Huot et al.): AUC-PR 11.5% persistence · 19.8% LR · 22.5% RF · 28.4% deep CNN on GPUs. Ours: 22.7% LR, 25.8% RF on a laptop CPU. Accuracy is 97–98% for every model, which is why we rank by AUC-PR and F1.

Comparative analysis

Precision–recall view

Boosted trees score highest in CV (0.83–0.85) but lower on test: overlapping crops of the same fire leak across CV folds and reward memorization. RF's averaging generalizes best.

Real-world application

Conclusion

Physics-informed features beat model complexity

  • Random Forest: F1 0.343, AUC-PR 0.258, 2.4× the persistence baseline
  • Engineered features add 59% AUC-PR; proximity to the fire front dominates (SHAP)
  • Digital twin: warming steadily increases next-day risk
  • Limitations: 1 km daily MODIS labels, one day of history, tile-level CV grouping
  • Next: multi-day inputs, event-level validation, test on Pakistani fires
